What Is Teeth Grinding (Bruxism)?

Bruxism is the involuntary grinding, clenching, or gnashing of the teeth. It is commonly associated with stress and anxiety and may occur during sleep or waking hours. Teeth grinding bruxism can contribute to enamel wear, jaw pain, and tooth sensitivity. A dental professional can assess signs of bruxism and recommend appropriate management options based on individual clinical findings.

What Causes Bruxism? The Role of Stress and Anxiety

Stress is one of the most frequently cited triggers for teeth grinding. When the body experiences psychological tension, it can manifest physically in many ways â€” and the jaw muscles are particularly susceptible. During periods of stress, you may unconsciously clench your jaw or grind your teeth, whether during the day or while asleep.

Research suggests that individuals experiencing work-related pressure, life changes, financial worries, or generalised anxiety are more likely to develop bruxism symptoms. The condition is also associated with certain lifestyle factors, including:

  • High caffeine or alcohol intake, which may increase muscle tension during sleep
  • Smoking, which has been linked to a higher incidence of bruxism
  • Sleep disturbances, including conditions such as obstructive sleep apnoea
  • Certain medications, particularly some types of antidepressants

It is worth noting that bruxism may have multiple contributing factors, and stress is not always the sole cause. In some cases, bite misalignment or other dental factors may play a role. A thorough clinical assessment is the most reliable way to identify the underlying contributors and determine the most suitable approach to management.

Recognising the Symptoms of Teeth Grinding

Many people who grind their teeth are unaware they are doing it, particularly if it occurs during sleep. Often, it is a partner who first notices the sound of grinding, or a dentist who identifies the physical signs during a routine examination.

Common symptoms and signs of bruxism include:

  • Jaw pain or stiffness, especially upon waking
  • Dull headaches, frequently felt around the temples
  • Tooth sensitivity, particularly to hot, cold, or sweet foods
  • Flattened, chipped, or worn tooth surfaces
  • Soreness in the facial muscles
  • Earache or pain near the ear (without an ear infection)
  • Disrupted sleep or reports from a partner of audible grinding sounds
  • Indentations on the tongue or inner cheeks

These symptoms may come and go, often worsening during stressful periods. If you notice any of these signs persisting, it may be helpful to discuss them with a dental professional who can carry out a detailed examination.

How Bruxism Affects Your Teeth and Jaw: The Dental Science Explained

To understand why teeth grinding can be harmful, it helps to know a little about tooth structure. Each tooth is protected by enamel â€” the hardest substance in the human body. Beneath the enamel lies dentine, a softer layer that contains microscopic tubules connected to the nerve of the tooth.

When bruxism occurs repeatedly over time, the forces generated during grinding can gradually wear down the enamel. The average human bite can exert significant pressure, and during grinding episodes â€” particularly during sleep â€” this force can be even greater than during normal chewing because the usual protective reflexes are reduced.

As enamel thins, the underlying dentine becomes more exposed. This is one of the reasons bruxism is associated with increased tooth sensitivity. In more advanced cases, the structural integrity of the teeth may be compromised, potentially leading to:

  • Microfractures or cracks in the tooth structure
  • Increased risk of chipping or breaking teeth
  • Strain on existing dental restorations such as crowns or fillings
  • Temporomandibular joint (TMJ) dysfunction, which can cause pain, clicking, or limited jaw movement

The jaw joint itself can be affected by the repeated excessive forces. The muscles responsible for chewing â€” particularly the masseter and temporalis muscles â€” may become overworked and tender, contributing to facial pain and headaches.

Understanding these effects highlights why early identification and management of bruxism can be beneficial for long-term oral health.

How Stress-Related Bruxism May Be Managed

There is no single solution that works for everyone, and the most appropriate approach depends on the individual's clinical presentation, the severity of the grinding, and the contributing factors involved. Management of teeth grinding bruxism typically involves a combination of dental and lifestyle strategies.

Dental Approaches

  • Custom-made occlusal splints (night guards): A dental professional may recommend a bespoke mouth guard, designed to be worn during sleep. These splints are made from impressions of your teeth and help to cushion the biting surfaces, reduce grinding forces, and protect the enamel. Over-the-counter options are available, but a custom-fitted splint from your dentist tends to offer better comfort, fit, and protection.

  • Assessment and adjustment of bite alignment: In some cases, an uneven bite may contribute to bruxism. Your dentist can assess whether this is a factor and discuss options if appropriate.

  • Restorative treatment for damaged teeth: If bruxism has already caused wear, chipping, or damage to existing dental restorations, your dentist may recommend treatment to restore tooth structure and protect against further deterioration.

Stress Management Strategies

Since stress plays a central role in many cases of bruxism, addressing the underlying tension can be a valuable part of the management plan:

  • Relaxation techniques such as mindfulness, deep breathing exercises, or progressive muscle relaxation before bed
  • Regular physical exercise, which is well evidenced as a means of reducing stress and improving sleep quality
  • Cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT), which may be beneficial for individuals whose bruxism is closely linked to anxiety
  • Improved sleep hygiene, including maintaining a consistent sleep schedule, reducing screen time before bed, and creating a calm sleeping environment

Jaw Exercises and Physiotherapy

Gentle jaw stretching exercises and physiotherapy techniques may help relieve muscle tension in the jaw. Your dentist or a specialist physiotherapist can advise on suitable exercises tailored to your needs.

When Professional Dental Assessment May Be Needed

Occasional jaw clenching â€” particularly during a stressful day â€” is common and not necessarily a cause for concern. However, there are situations where seeking a dental assessment may be appropriate:

  • Persistent jaw pain or stiffness that does not resolve within a few days
  • Noticeable tooth wear, flattening, or chipping
  • Ongoing tooth sensitivity that develops without an obvious cause
  • Frequent morning headaches or facial muscle soreness
  • A partner reporting regular grinding sounds during sleep
  • Difficulty opening or closing the jaw, or a clicking or locking sensation

A dental professional can examine the teeth, jaw joints, and surrounding muscles to assess whether bruxism is present and determine whether any damage has occurred. Early identification allows for a proactive approach, which can help reduce the risk of more significant problems developing over time.

If you are experiencing any of these symptoms, a general dental check-up provides an opportunity to discuss your concerns and receive tailored advice.

Prevention and Oral Health Advice

While it may not always be possible to prevent bruxism entirely, there are practical steps that can help reduce the frequency and impact of teeth grinding:

  • Be mindful of jaw clenching during the day. Try to notice when you are clenching and consciously relax the jaw, keeping the lips together but the teeth slightly apart.
  • Limit stimulants in the evening. Reducing caffeine and alcohol intake, particularly in the hours before bed, may help reduce nocturnal grinding.
  • Establish a calming bedtime routine. Relaxation activities such as reading, warm baths, or gentle stretching can signal to the body that it is time to unwind.
  • Avoid chewing on non-food items. Habits such as chewing pens, ice, or fingernails can encourage the jaw muscles to remain in a clenching pattern.
  • Stay hydrated and maintain a balanced diet. General wellbeing supports muscle function, including the muscles of the jaw.
  • Attend regular dental appointments. Routine check-ups allow your dentist to monitor for early signs of wear or damage, even before you may notice symptoms yourself. Maintaining good dental hygiene is also important for overall oral health, particularly if enamel wear has already begun.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can teeth grinding cause permanent damage to my teeth?

Over time, untreated bruxism may cause noticeable enamel wear, increased tooth sensitivity, and in some cases, cracks or chips in the teeth. Existing dental restorations such as crowns or fillings may also be affected. However, with early identification and appropriate management â€” such as the use of a custom-made occlusal splint â€” the risk of significant damage can be reduced. Your dentist can assess the extent of any wear and recommend suitable protective or restorative options based on your individual circumstances.

How do I know if I grind my teeth at night?

Nocturnal bruxism can be difficult to self-diagnose because it occurs during sleep. Common indicators include waking with a sore jaw, morning headaches, or facial muscle tenderness. A partner may also notice grinding sounds during the night. Your dentist can identify physical signs of grinding during a routine examination, such as flattened tooth surfaces or patterns of enamel wear. If you suspect you may be grinding your teeth, discussing your symptoms with a dental professional is a helpful first step.

Are over-the-counter night guards effective for bruxism?

Over-the-counter mouth guards may offer some degree of protection; however, they are not custom-fitted and may be less comfortable, potentially affecting sleep quality. A custom-made occlusal splint, fabricated by your dentist from precise impressions of your teeth, provides a more accurate fit, better protection, and greater comfort. Your dentist can advise whether a custom appliance would be appropriate for your situation based on the severity of your bruxism and any existing dental concerns.

Can children develop bruxism?

Yes, teeth grinding can occur in children as well as adults. It is relatively common in younger children and may be related to the development of their teeth and jaws. In many cases, childhood bruxism resolves naturally as the child grows. However, if a child is experiencing jaw pain, headaches, or noticeable tooth wear, it is advisable to mention this to their dentist during a routine appointment so that the situation can be monitored.

Will reducing stress definitely stop my teeth grinding?

Stress management can be a very effective component of bruxism management, particularly when stress or anxiety is a primary contributing factor. However, bruxism may have multiple causes, and reducing stress alone may not fully resolve the condition in every case. A comprehensive approach â€” combining stress reduction with dental protective measures â€” tends to be the most effective strategy. Your dentist can help identify the factors most relevant to your situation.

Is bruxism linked to other health conditions?

Bruxism has been associated with several other conditions, including sleep apnoea, gastro-oesophageal reflux, and certain anxiety disorders. Some medications, particularly selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs), have also been linked to an increased risk of teeth grinding. If you are concerned about a possible connection between bruxism and another health condition or medication, it is advisable to discuss this with both your dentist and your GP for a coordinated approach to management.
