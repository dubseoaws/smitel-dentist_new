What Is a Gummy Smile?

How does gum contouring fix a gummy smile?

Gum contouring is a cosmetic dental procedure that carefully reshapes excess gum tissue to reveal more of the tooth's natural crown. By adjusting the gum line, gum contouring can help create a more balanced and proportionate appearance when smiling. Treatment suitability depends on a thorough clinical assessment of each patient's oral health and gum tissue.

What Causes a Gummy Smile?

A gummy smile can result from several different factors, and in many cases, more than one cause may be present. Understanding the underlying reason is an important part of determining which treatment approach may be most appropriate.

Excess gum tissue growth is one of the most common causes. Some individuals naturally develop more gingival tissue than others, which can cover a larger portion of the tooth surface and make teeth appear shorter than they actually are. This is sometimes referred to as altered passive eruption, where the gum tissue has not receded to its expected position during dental development.

Skeletal and jaw development can also play a role. If the upper jaw (maxilla) has grown longer than average, more gum tissue may become visible during smiling, even when the gum and teeth themselves are perfectly healthy.

Lip movement and muscle activity is another contributing factor. Some people have a hyperactive upper lip that rises higher than usual when smiling, revealing more of the gum line.

Tooth size and eruption patterns may also contribute. If teeth are naturally smaller or have not fully erupted through the gum tissue, the visible ratio of gum to tooth may appear unbalanced.

A clinical examination is necessary to identify the specific cause or combination of causes in each patient.

Understanding the Anatomy Behind a Gummy Smile

To appreciate how gum contouring works, it helps to understand the basic anatomy of the teeth and surrounding soft tissues.

Each tooth has two main parts: the crown, which is the visible portion above the gum line, and the root, which sits within the jawbone. The gum tissue â€” also called the gingiva â€” surrounds and supports the base of each tooth, forming a protective seal against bacteria and debris.

In a balanced smile, the gum tissue sits just above the point where the enamel of the tooth meets the root surface. This junction is known as the cementoenamel junction (CEJ). When the gum tissue covers more of the enamel than expected, the teeth can appear shorter, and more pink tissue becomes visible during smiling.

The bone surrounding the tooth roots â€” called the alveolar bone â€” also plays a role. In some cases, the bone level may sit higher than expected, supporting excess gum tissue. This is important because any gum contouring procedure needs to consider the relationship between the gum, the tooth, and the underlying bone to achieve a stable and healthy result.

Understanding this anatomy helps explain why a thorough clinical assessment, often including dental radiographs, is essential before any cosmetic gum treatment is considered.

How Does Gum Contouring Work?

Gum contouring, also known as gingivectomy or gum reshaping, is a cosmetic dental procedure designed to remove or reshape excess gum tissue to create a more even and proportionate gum line.

The procedure is typically carried out using a soft tissue laser or a surgical scalpel, depending on the clinical situation and the dentist's assessment. Laser gum contouring has become increasingly popular as it can offer precise tissue removal, reduced bleeding, and may support a more comfortable healing process for many patients.

During the procedure, a local anaesthetic is administered to ensure patient comfort. The dentist then carefully removes or sculpts the excess gum tissue, following a planned outline that has been discussed with the patient beforehand. In some cases, a small amount of bone may also need to be adjusted â€” a procedure known as crown lengthening â€” to achieve a stable, long-lasting result.

Treatment duration varies depending on the extent of reshaping required. For minor adjustments, the procedure may take less than an hour. More complex cases involving multiple teeth or bone adjustment may require a longer appointment or staged treatment.

It is important to note that results can vary between individuals, and a detailed clinical examination is needed to determine whether gum contouring is the most suitable approach. Your dentist will discuss the expected outcomes, any limitations, and alternative options during your cosmetic dentistry consultation.

What to Expect During Recovery

Recovery following gum contouring is generally straightforward, though individual experiences may vary depending on the extent of the procedure and each patient's healing response.

In the first few days after treatment, it is normal to experience some tenderness, mild swelling, and sensitivity around the treated areas. These symptoms typically improve within a week. Over-the-counter pain relief, such as paracetamol or ibuprofen (as directed by your dentist or pharmacist), is usually sufficient to manage any discomfort.

Patients are generally advised to:

  • Eat soft, cool foods for the first few days
  • Avoid spicy, acidic, or crunchy foods that may irritate healing gum tissue
  • Maintain gentle oral hygiene, brushing carefully around the treated area
  • Use any prescribed antimicrobial mouthwash as directed
  • Avoid smoking, as this can impair healing

The gum tissue usually heals over the course of two to four weeks, though the final appearance of the gum line may take a little longer to settle. Follow-up appointments allow your dentist to monitor healing and ensure the tissue is responding well.

If you experience significant bleeding, increasing pain, or signs of infection following any dental procedure, it is important to contact your dental practice promptly.

Is Gum Contouring Suitable for Everyone?

Gum contouring can be an effective option for many patients, but it is not appropriate in every case. Suitability depends on a number of clinical factors that can only be determined through a thorough examination.

Patients with healthy gum tissue and adequate bone support are generally considered good candidates for the procedure. However, if there are signs of active gum disease, untreated decay, or insufficient bone levels, these issues would typically need to be addressed before any cosmetic reshaping could be considered.

In cases where the gummy smile is primarily caused by jaw position or skeletal factors, gum contouring alone may not achieve the desired result. Orthodontic treatment or, in some situations, surgical jaw correction may be more appropriate options to discuss with a specialist.

Similarly, if a hyperactive lip muscle is the main contributing factor, treatments such as lip repositioning or muscle relaxation techniques may be considered alongside or instead of gum contouring.

Your dentist will assess your individual anatomy, oral health, and aesthetic goals to recommend the most suitable approach. It is important to have realistic expectations and to understand that outcomes may vary. A dental health check is an ideal starting point for exploring your options.

Prevention and Oral Health Advice

While a gummy smile is often related to natural anatomy or developmental factors rather than something that can be prevented, maintaining excellent oral health is always beneficial for the long-term appearance and function of your gums.

Good oral hygiene is the foundation of healthy gum tissue. Brushing twice daily with a fluoride toothpaste, using interdental brushes or floss to clean between teeth, and attending regular dental check-ups all help to keep gums in optimal condition.

Gum inflammation, if left untreated, can lead to changes in the appearance of the gum tissue. Swollen or inflamed gums may appear more prominent, which can accentuate a gummy smile. Maintaining healthy gums through good hygiene and professional cleaning can help manage this.

Medication-related gum changes are another consideration. Certain medications â€” including some anti-seizure drugs, calcium channel blockers, and immunosuppressants â€” can cause gum tissue overgrowth (gingival hyperplasia). If you notice changes in your gums after starting a new medication, discuss this with your dentist or GP.

Regular dental visits allow your dental team to monitor your gum health, identify any changes early, and provide tailored advice to keep your mouth healthy. Prevention and early intervention remain the cornerstones of good dental care.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is gum contouring painful?

Gum contouring is carried out under local anaesthetic, so the procedure itself should not be painful. After the anaesthetic wears off, some tenderness and mild discomfort are common for a few days. Most patients find this manageable with over-the-counter pain relief. Laser-assisted techniques may reduce post-operative discomfort compared to traditional surgical methods, though individual experiences vary. Your dentist will discuss pain management options with you before and after the procedure to ensure you feel as comfortable as possible throughout.

How long do the results of gum contouring last?

When performed correctly and supported by good oral health, the results of gum contouring are generally long-lasting. The removed gum tissue does not typically grow back. However, factors such as ongoing gum disease, medication-related gum changes, or poor oral hygiene may affect the long-term appearance of the gum line. Maintaining regular dental visits and a thorough home care routine helps to preserve the results. Your dentist will advise on any specific aftercare steps relevant to your individual situation.

Can gum contouring fix uneven gums?

Gum contouring can be an effective approach for addressing an uneven gum line, where some teeth appear longer or shorter than others due to inconsistent gum tissue levels. By carefully reshaping the tissue, a dentist can create a more symmetrical and proportionate gum line. However, the suitability and extent of correction depend on the underlying anatomy, including the position of the bone and the health of the gum tissue. A clinical assessment will help determine what level of improvement may be achievable.

Are there risks associated with gum contouring?

As with any dental or surgical procedure, gum contouring carries some risks. These may include infection, prolonged sensitivity, bleeding, or an uneven result. In rare cases, damage to the tooth root or surrounding structures may occur. Choosing a qualified and experienced dental professional helps to minimise these risks. Your dentist will explain the potential risks and benefits during your consultation, allowing you to make an informed decision about whether to proceed with treatment.

How much does gum contouring cost in the UK?

The cost of gum contouring in the UK varies depending on the complexity of the case, the number of teeth involved, and the technique used. As a cosmetic procedure, gum contouring is not typically available on the NHS and is usually provided privately. During your consultation, your dentist will provide a personalised treatment plan that includes clear information about fees. It is advisable to discuss costs, payment options, and what the treatment fee covers before committing to any procedure.

Can I have gum contouring if I have gum disease?

Active gum disease would generally need to be treated and stabilised before any cosmetic gum procedure could be considered. Gum contouring performed on unhealthy tissue may lead to complications, delayed healing, or unsatisfactory results. Your dentist will assess the health of your gums thoroughly and, if necessary, recommend a course of periodontal treatment before discussing cosmetic options. Addressing gum disease first supports both your oral health and the likelihood of a successful cosmetic outcome.
