What Counts as a Dental Emergency?

Understanding whether your situation requires urgent attention can help you respond appropriately. While only a dental professional can provide a definitive diagnosis, certain symptoms and situations are generally considered to warrant prompt assessment.

Severe or Persistent Toothache

Toothache can range from mild sensitivity to intense, persistent pain. When pain is severe, continuous, or worsening despite over-the-counter pain relief, it may indicate an underlying issue that requires professional attention. Pain that disrupts sleep, prevents eating, or significantly affects your quality of life generally warrants assessment sooner rather than later.

Facial Swelling or Suspected Infection

Swelling in the face, jaw, or gums can sometimes indicate an infection. When swelling is accompanied by pain, fever, difficulty swallowing, or a general feeling of being unwell, it's important to seek professional advice promptly. Dental infections require appropriate clinical assessment and management.

Broken, Cracked, or Knocked-Out Teeth

Trauma to the teethâ€”whether from an accident, sports injury, or biting on something unexpectedly hardâ€”can result in chips, cracks, or complete tooth loss. The urgency of treatment often depends on the extent of the damage and whether the nerve or surrounding tissues are affected.

If a tooth has been completely knocked out (avulsed), time can be a factor in determining whether it can be successfully replanted. Handling the tooth carefully and seeking prompt professional advice is generally recommended.

Bleeding or Trauma

Bleeding from the mouth that doesn't stop after applying gentle pressure for 15-20 minutes, or significant trauma to the teeth, gums, or jaw, may require urgent assessment. This includes injuries from falls, impacts, or accidents that affect the mouth area.

Important Note: If you experience difficulty breathing, severe swelling affecting your airway, or symptoms that suggest a medical emergency, please contact emergency medical services (999) immediately.

Remember that self-diagnosis has limitations. If you're unsure whether your situation constitutes an emergency, contacting a dental practice for guidance is often the most sensible approach. An emergency dentist in London can help assess your situation and advise on appropriate next steps.

What to Do Immediately Before You See a Dentist

While professional assessment is essential, there are some steps you can take to manage your comfort and protect your teeth while you arrange to see a dentist.

Safe Steps to Manage Pain or Swelling

  • Over-the-counter pain relief: If appropriate for you and you have no contraindications, standard pain relief such as paracetamol or ibuprofen may help manage discomfort temporarily. Always follow the dosage instructions on the packaging
  • Cold compress: Applying a cold pack wrapped in a cloth to the outside of your cheek may help reduce swelling and provide some comfort. Apply for intervals of 15-20 minutes
  • Rinse gently: A gentle rinse with warm salt water can help keep the area clean and may provide temporary relief for some types of discomfort
  • Keep your head elevated: If lying down, propping your head up with pillows may help reduce throbbing sensations
  • Avoid very hot or cold foods and drinks: Extreme temperatures can sometimes aggravate tooth sensitivity and pain

What to Avoid Doing at Home

  • Avoid placing aspirin directly on teeth or gums: This can cause tissue burns and doesn't effectively relieve dental pain
  • Don't attempt to extract or move teeth yourself: This can cause additional damage and complications
  • Avoid ignoring persistent symptoms: While it's tempting to hope pain will resolve on its own, delaying assessment can sometimes allow problems to progress
  • Don't use sharp objects to probe or poke at teeth: This can cause injury or introduce bacteria
  • Avoid excessive alcohol as pain relief: While it may seem to help temporarily, alcohol can mask symptoms and isn't a substitute for proper pain management

For knocked-out teeth: If a permanent tooth has been completely knocked out, handle it only by the crown (the white part), not the root. If possible, gently rinse it with milk or saline and try to place it back in the socket. If that isn't possible, keep it moist in milk or saliva. Time may be important, so seek professional advice promptly.

Common Emergency Dental Treatments Explained

Emergency dental care focuses on addressing the immediate issue, relieving discomfort, and preventing further problems. The specific treatment recommended will depend on your individual situation, which can only be determined through proper clinical assessment.

Examination and Diagnosis

The first step in any emergency appointment is a thorough assessment. Your dentist will examine the affected area, ask about your symptoms and how they developed, and may take X-rays to understand what's happening beneath the surface. This diagnostic process is essential for determining the most appropriate course of action.

Temporary Stabilisation vs Definitive Care

Emergency treatment often involves two phases. The immediate priority is usually to address acute symptomsâ€”relieving pain, managing infection, or protecting damaged teeth from further harm. This might involve:

  • Prescribing appropriate medication if clinically indicated
  • Draining an abscess to relieve pressure
  • Placing a temporary filling or crown to protect a damaged tooth
  • Stabilising a loose or displaced tooth
  • Extracting a tooth that cannot be saved, if necessary

Following emergency stabilisation, you may need to return for definitive treatmentâ€”a permanent filling, crown, root canal treatment, or other procedure to fully resolve the underlying issue.

Focus on Relieving the Underlying Issue

Effective emergency care doesn't just mask symptomsâ€”it addresses the root cause of the problem. Your dentist will explain what's causing your discomfort, what treatment options are available, and what to expect during the recovery process. Every treatment plan is tailored to the individual patient and their specific circumstances.

When Is Emergency Care Necessary â€” and When Can It Wait?

Not every dental concern requires immediate emergency treatment, but it can be difficult to judge this without professional input. Here's some general guidance to help you consider your optionsâ€”though this doesn't replace proper clinical advice.

Situations That Generally Warrant Urgent Review

  • Severe pain that isn't manageable with over-the-counter pain relief
  • Significant facial swelling, especially if spreading or accompanied by fever
  • A tooth that has been knocked out or significantly displaced
  • Uncontrolled bleeding from the mouth
  • A broken tooth with sharp edges causing injury to the tongue or cheeks
  • Signs of infection such as pus, bad taste, or swollen lymph nodes

Situations That May Allow for Monitoring

  • Minor tooth sensitivity that comes and goes
  • Small chips without pain or sensitivity
  • A lost filling with no discomfort (though this should still be addressed soon)
  • Mild, occasional discomfort that doesn't affect daily activities

Frequently Asked Questions

What should I do if I have severe tooth pain at night?

If you're experiencing severe tooth pain outside of normal hours, try taking over-the-counter pain relief if appropriate for you, apply a cold compress to the outside of your cheek, and keep your head elevated. Contact a dental practice as soon as possible the next day, or seek out-of-hours emergency dental services if the pain is unbearable or accompanied by concerning symptoms like significant swelling.

Is a chipped tooth a dental emergency?

It depends on the extent of the damage. A small chip with no pain may not require immediate emergency care, though it should be assessed soon to prevent further damage. However, if the chip is large, causes pain, has sharp edges injuring your mouth, or exposes the inner part of the tooth, you should seek prompt professional assessment.

How quickly should I see a dentist for a knocked-out tooth?

Time can be a factor with knocked-out permanent teeth. Contact a dentist as soon as possible after the injury. In the meantime, handle the tooth by the crown only, keep it moist in milk or saliva, and if possible, try to gently place it back in the socket.

Can I wait to see my regular dentist instead of seeking emergency care?

This depends on your symptoms. For mild discomfort or minor issues, waiting for a routine appointment with your regular dentist is often appropriate. However, for severe pain, significant swelling, trauma, or concerning symptoms, seeking earlier assessment is generally advisable. When in doubt, call your dental practice for guidance.

What happens at an emergency dental appointment?

Your dentist will first assess your condition through examination and possibly X-rays. They'll explain what they find, discuss treatment options, and provide clear information about any associated costs. Emergency treatment focuses on addressing immediate concernsâ€”relieving pain, managing infection, or stabilising damaged teeth. Follow-up treatment may be needed to fully resolve the issue.

Should I go to A&E for a dental emergency?

Hospital A&E departments are generally not equipped for dental treatment and will usually refer you to a dentist. However, you should attend A&E if you have difficulty breathing, severe facial swelling affecting your airway, heavy bleeding that won't stop, or injuries involving facial fractures or other medical concerns alongside dental problems.

How can I manage dental anxiety during an emergency?

Dental anxiety is very common, and it can feel heightened during stressful emergency situations. Let your dental team know about your concernsâ€”they're experienced in helping anxious patients and can explain what to expect at each stage. Focusing on your breathing, bringing a supportive friend or family member, and remembering that treatment will help resolve your pain can all help.
