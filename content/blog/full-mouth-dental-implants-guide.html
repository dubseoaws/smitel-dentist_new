What Are Full Mouth Dental Implants?

Full mouth dental implants refer to implant-supported restorations that replace all or most of the teeth in one or both dental arches. Rather than placing an individual implant for every missing tooth, a carefully planned number of implants are positioned along the jawbone to support a full set of fixed replacement teeth.

The restoration is typically a fixed bridge or framework that is secured directly to the implants, meaning it stays in place and is not designed to be removed by the patient for cleaning or overnight. This distinguishes full mouth implants from traditional removable dentures, which rest on the gums and often require adhesive.

Full mouth implant solutions are designed to replicate the appearance and function of natural teeth as closely as possible, providing patients with a stable, comfortable restoration that supports everyday activities like eating, speaking, and smiling.

How Full Mouth Implant Treatment Works

Full mouth implant treatment is a multi-phase process that requires careful planning and typically takes several months to complete. While every patient's journey is different, the general treatment pathway includes the following stages:

  • 1.Assessment and planning: A thorough evaluation of your oral health, including your gums, remaining teeth, and jawbone condition. This informs the treatment approach, the number of implants needed, and the type of restoration most suitable for your situation.
  • 2.Preparatory treatment: If necessary, any existing dental issues â€” such as gum disease or teeth requiring extraction â€” are addressed before implant placement begins.
  • 3.Implant placement: The implants are surgically positioned in the jawbone under local anaesthetic. The number and placement of implants depends on the clinical plan and the type of restoration being supported.
  • 4.Healing and integration: A healing period follows, during which the implants integrate with the surrounding bone â€” a process called osseointegration. Temporary restorations may be provided during this phase to maintain function and appearance.
  • 5.Final restoration: Once healing is complete and the implants are secure, the definitive prosthetic teeth are fabricated and fitted to the implants.

Different Full Arch Implant Approaches

There are several approaches to full arch implant treatment, and the most appropriate one depends on your bone support, oral health, and the design of the final restoration:

  • Implant-supported bridges: A fixed bridge framework is attached to multiple implants along the arch. This provides a stable, non-removable set of replacement teeth that distributes chewing forces across the supporting implants.
  • Fixed full arch restorations: A complete arch of teeth is secured to a planned number of implants. The prosthesis is designed as a single unit that is fixed in place and not intended for removal, offering a natural appearance and strong functional support.
  • Implant-retained overdentures: For patients who prefer a removable option with improved stability, implant-supported dentures clip onto implants for a more secure fit than conventional dentures while still allowing removal for cleaning.

Each approach has its own advantages, and your dental team will discuss which option may be most suitable for your clinical situation and personal preferences.

How Dentists Plan Full Mouth Implant Treatment

Evaluating oral health: Your dentist will examine the condition of your gums, any remaining teeth, and the overall health of your mouth. Active gum disease or other dental issues are typically addressed before implant treatment begins.

Assessing bone support: Adequate jawbone volume and density are essential for secure implant placement. Maintenance and Long-Term Care

Full mouth implant restorations require ongoing care to remain healthy and functional. While implants cannot develop cavities, the gum and bone tissue around them can become inflamed without proper hygiene.

Daily cleaning: Brushing twice daily with a soft-bristled toothbrush is essential. Interdental brushes, water flossers, and floss threaders are particularly helpful for cleaning around and beneath fixed implant restorations where food particles can collect. For a detailed guide, see our article on how to clean dental implants.

Professional maintenance: Regular hygiene appointments allow your dental team to professionally clean around the implants using specialised instruments, monitor the health of the surrounding tissue, and identify any issues early.

Protecting your restoration: Avoiding excessive biting forces on very hard foods, wearing a mouthguard if you grind your teeth, and attending regular dental reviews all contribute to the long-term care of your implant-supported teeth.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many implants are needed for a full mouth?

The number of implants depends on the clinical approach, your bone support, and the type of restoration planned. Full arch restorations typically use several implants per jaw, but the exact number is determined during the assessment and planning stage.

Are full mouth dental implants permanent?

Full mouth implant restorations are designed as fixed, long-term restorations. The implants integrate with the jawbone and the prosthetic teeth are secured in place. However, the prosthetic components may require maintenance, adjustments, or replacement over time due to normal wear.

How long does full mouth implant treatment take?

Treatment typically spans several months, including the healing period required for the implants to integrate with the jawbone. The exact timeline depends on whether preparatory procedures are needed and how the healing progresses. Your dental team will provide estimated timescales during planning.

How much do full mouth implants cost in the UK?

Costs vary significantly depending on the number of implants, the type of restoration, any preparatory treatments needed, and the complexity of the case. A consultation and clinical assessment are necessary to provide an accurate, personalised treatment plan and cost breakdown.

Can implants replace dentures completely?

Yes. Many patients transition from removable dentures to fixed implant-supported restorations. Implants can also be used to stabilise existing dentures for improved fit and comfort. Your dental team will assess your suitability and discuss the options available to you.

How do you clean full mouth dental implants?

Full mouth implant restorations are cleaned with a soft-bristled toothbrush, interdental brushes, and water flossers. Cleaning beneath fixed bridge frameworks is particularly important. Regular professional hygiene appointments complement your daily routine and help maintain the health of the surrounding tissue.

Is full mouth implant treatment painful?

Implant placement is carried out under local anaesthetic, so you should not feel pain during the procedure. Some discomfort, swelling, or bruising may occur in the days following surgery, but this is typically manageable and resolves within a short period.

Dental symptoms and treatment options should always be assessed individually during a clinical examination.
