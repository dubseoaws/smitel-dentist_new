What Is a Root Canal?

If your dentist has recommended root canal treatment, you're certainly not alone. This common dental procedure helps millions of people each year keep their natural teeth and relieve the pain caused by infection or damage.

In simple terms, a root canal is a treatment designed to save a tooth that has become badly decayed or infected. Inside every tooth, beneath the white enamel and hard layer called dentine, lies a soft tissue known as the pulp. This pulp contains nerves, blood vessels, and connective tissue that helped your tooth grow during development.

When this inner pulp becomes inflamed or infectedâ€”whether through deep decay, a crack, or injuryâ€”it can cause significant pain and, if left untreated, lead to an abscess or tooth loss.

Root canal treatment removes the damaged pulp, cleans and disinfects the inside of the tooth, then fills and seals it. The goal is straightforward: to eliminate infection, relieve pain, and preserve your natural tooth for years to come.

Why You Might Need a Root Canal

Several situations can lead to pulp damage and the need for root canal treatment. Understanding these causes can help you recognise when to seek care.

Common Causes of Pulp Damage

  • Deep decay: When a cavity progresses beyond the enamel and dentine, bacteria can reach the pulp and cause infection
  • Cracked or chipped teeth: A fracture can expose the pulp to bacteria, even if the crack isn't visible
  • Repeated dental procedures: Multiple treatments on the same tooth can stress the pulp over time
  • Trauma or injury: A blow to the mouth can damage the pulp, sometimes without any visible signs on the tooth's surface
  • Large fillings: Extensive restorations may leave little protection for the underlying pulp

Signs You May Need Treatment

Watch for these symptoms, which may indicate pulp damage:

  • Persistent or severe toothache, especially when chewing or applying pressure
  • Prolonged sensitivity to hot or cold temperatures that lingers after the source is removed
  • Darkening or discolouration of the tooth
  • Swelling and tenderness in the nearby gums
  • A recurring spot or pimple on the gums (which may indicate an abscess)

If you notice any of these signs, we recommend booking a dental check-up promptly. Early assessment gives you the best chance of saving the tooth.

How Root Canal Treatment Works: A Step-by-Step Guide

Understanding what happens during root canal treatment can help ease any concerns. Here's what to expect at each stage:

1. Diagnosis and X-rays

Your dentist will first examine your tooth and take X-rays to assess the extent of infection and see the shape of the root canals. This helps plan the most effective treatment approach.

2. Local Anaesthetic

Before treatment begins, your dentist will administer a local anaesthetic to numb the area around the affected tooth. Most patients feel little to no discomfort during the procedure â€” typically just some pressure or movement. If you experience discomfort at any point, your dentist can adjust the anaesthesia.

3. Accessing the Pulp

Once you're comfortable, your dentist will place a protective sheet (rubber dam) around the tooth to keep it clean and dry. A small opening is then made in the crown of the tooth to access the pulp chamber.

4. Removing Infected Tissue

Using specialised instruments, your dentist carefully removes the infected or damaged pulp tissue from the pulp chamber and root canals. This step eliminates the source of infection and pain.

5. Cleaning and Shaping

The empty canals are thoroughly cleaned, disinfected, and shaped to prepare them for filling. Your dentist may use irrigation solutions to flush away debris and bacteria.

6. Filling and Sealing

The cleaned canals are filled with a biocompatible material called gutta-percha, which is placed with adhesive cement to ensure a complete seal. This prevents bacteria from re-entering.

7. Final Restoration

After the root canal is complete, your tooth will need a permanent restorationâ€”usually a crownâ€”to protect it and restore full function. A tooth that has had root canal treatment can become more brittle, so a crown provides essential reinforcement.

Good to know: Root canal treatment is often completed in one or two appointments, depending on the complexity of your case. Your dentist will explain the expected timeline during your consultation.

Does a Root Canal Hurt?

This is perhaps the most common concern patients haveâ€”and understandably so. The good news is that modern root canal treatment is designed to relieve pain, not cause it.

Modern Pain Management

Today's dental techniques and anaesthetics have greatly improved the root canal experience. Your dentist will administer local anaesthetic before beginning, and many patients report that the procedure feels similar to having a standard filling.

What You Might Feel

  • During treatment: Most patients feel pressure and movement but no sharp pain. If you feel discomfort at any point, let your dentist knowâ€”additional anaesthetic can be provided
  • After treatment: Some tenderness or mild discomfort is normal for a few days. This typically responds well to over-the-counter pain relief

Pain From Infection vs Treatment

It's worth noting that the pain people associate with root canals often comes from the infection itselfâ€”not the treatment. In fact, most patients feel significant relief once the infected tissue is removed. The procedure is the solution to pain, not the cause.

Root Canal Myths vs Facts

There's a lot of misinformation about root canal treatment. Let's address some common misconceptions:

âŒ Myth: "Root canals are extremely painful"

Fact: With modern anaesthetics and techniques, root canal treatment is typically no more uncomfortable than getting a filling. The procedure actually relieves the pain caused by infection.

âŒ Myth: "It's better to just have the tooth extracted"

Fact: Preserving your natural tooth is almost always preferable. Tooth extraction leads to bone loss and may require replacement options like implants or bridges, which involve additional procedures and cost.

âŒ Myth: "Root canal treatment causes illness"

Fact: This outdated claim has been thoroughly debunked by scientific research. Root canal treatment is a safe, well-established procedure that removes infectionâ€”it doesn't cause health problems.

âŒ Myth: "If my tooth doesn't hurt, I don't need treatment"

Fact: A tooth can have a dead or dying nerve without causing immediate pain. Regular dental check-ups help identify problems early, before they become emergencies.

âŒ Myth: "The tooth will need to be removed eventually anyway"

Fact: With proper care, a tooth that has had root canal treatment can last many years, and in some cases, a lifetime. Published clinical research generally reports favourable outcomes when the tooth is properly restored and maintained, though individual results depend on a range of factors.

Recovery Tips After Root Canal Treatment

Following your root canal, proper aftercare helps ensure the best outcome. Here's what to keep in mind:

Managing Discomfort

  • Some tenderness is normal for a few daysâ€”this usually improves steadily
  • Over-the-counter pain relievers like paracetamol or ibuprofen can help manage any discomfort
  • Avoid chewing on the treated side until any temporary filling is replaced with a permanent restoration

Eating and Drinking

  • Wait until the numbness wears off completely before eating to avoid accidentally biting your cheek or tongue
  • Stick to soft foods for the first day or two
  • Avoid very hot or cold foods if you experience sensitivity
  • Don't chew directly on the treated tooth until it has been fully restored with a crown

Oral Hygiene

  • Continue brushing and flossing as normal, being gentle around the treated area
  • Rinse gently with warm salt water if recommended by your dentist
  • Attend your follow-up appointment to have the permanent restoration placed

When to Contact Your Dentist

While some discomfort is normal, contact your dental practice if you experience:

  • Severe pain that doesn't improve with medication
  • Visible swelling inside or outside your mouth
  • An allergic reaction to medication (rash, itching, difficulty breathing)
  • Your bite feels uneven or the temporary filling comes loose
  • Symptoms that return after initial improvement

When to See Your Dentist

Early intervention gives you the best chance of saving a damaged tooth. Book an appointment if you notice:

  • Persistent tooth pain that doesn't go away
  • Sensitivity to hot or cold that lingers long after exposure
  • A tooth that has become noticeably darker
  • Swelling or tenderness in your gums
  • A recurring bump or "pimple" near a tooth
  • Pain when biting or touching a specific tooth

If you're experiencing severe pain, significant swelling, or symptoms outside normal hours, you may need emergency dental care.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does a root canal take?

Most root canal treatments take between 60 to 90 minutes, though this can vary depending on the tooth's complexity. Front teeth are often quicker to treat than molars, which have multiple roots.

How much does root canal treatment cost in the UK?

Costs vary depending on whether you're treated privately or on the NHS, and which tooth requires treatment. Your dentist will provide a clear breakdown of costs before treatment begins.

Can I drive home after a root canal?

Yes, in most cases. Root canal treatment uses local anaesthetic, which numbs only the treatment area. You should be fine to drive once the numbness begins to wear off.

How long does it take to recover from a root canal?

Most patients feel back to normal within a few days. Any tenderness typically subsides within a week. You'll need a follow-up appointment for your permanent restoration.

Is a crown always needed after root canal treatment?

In most cases, yesâ€”especially for back teeth. A crown protects the tooth from fracture and restores its strength. Your dentist will advise on the best restoration for your specific situation.

What happens if I don't get a root canal when I need one?

Without treatment, the infection can spread, potentially leading to an abscess, bone loss, or the need for extraction. In rare cases, untreated dental infections can affect overall health.

Can a root canal fail?

While root canal treatment is a well-established procedure with generally favourable outcomes, occasionally a tooth may not heal as expected or develop new problems. If this occurs, retreatment or other options may help preserve the tooth.
