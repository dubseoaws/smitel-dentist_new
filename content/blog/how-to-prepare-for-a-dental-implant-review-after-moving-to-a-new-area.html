Moving to a new part of the country is exciting, but it often raises an important question that many patients overlook until something feels wrong: who will look after my dental implants now? If you have one or more dental implants, maintaining regular professional reviews is a central part of keeping them healthy and functioning well for years to come. When you relocate, finding a new dental provider and ensuring continuity of care becomes a genuine priority.

Many people search online for guidance on dental implant aftercare after moving because they are unsure what records they need, what a new dentist will want to assess, and whether their implants are still in good condition. This article explains what a dental implant review typically involves, how to prepare for an appointment with a new dental practice, what information to bring along, and why ongoing monitoring matters. It also explains when it may be appropriate to seek a professional dental assessment sooner rather than later. What Should You Do About a Dental Implant Review After Moving?

To prepare for a dental implant review after moving, gather your previous dental records, implant documentation, and any treatment summaries from your former dentist. Contact a new dental practice to register and request a full assessment. Dental implant aftercare requires regular professional monitoring to check implant stability, gum health, and the condition of the restoration.

Why Dental Implant Aftercare Matters Long Term

Dental implants are designed to be a long-term tooth replacement solution, and with appropriate care, many people enjoy the benefits of their implants for many years. However, implants are not entirely maintenance-free. The bone and gum tissue around an implant require ongoing monitoring, and the crown or restoration attached to the implant may occasionally need adjustment or replacement.

One of the most important aspects of long-term implant success is professional review. During these appointments, a dentist or implant clinician can assess the soft tissue surrounding the implant, check the stability of the implant itself, evaluate the bone levels using dental X-rays, and examine the condition of the prosthetic crown or bridge.

When patients move to a new area and experience a gap in their dental care, there is a risk that early signs of complications — such as changes in the surrounding gum tissue or minor bone changes — go undetected for longer than they otherwise would. This is not a reason for alarm, but it does underline why re-establishing dental care with a new practice should be treated as a priority rather than something to address only when a problem arises.

If you have had dental implants placed, you will likely already appreciate how carefully the procedure was planned. Maintaining that investment through consistent aftercare is simply the next chapter of that process.

What Records and Documentation Should You Bring?

One of the most practical steps you can take before attending a dental implant review at a new practice is to gather as much relevant documentation as possible. This helps a new clinician understand your implant history and provide the most informed assessment.

Useful documents and information to collect include:

  • Implant treatment records — ideally a summary letter or treatment notes from your previous dental practice
  • Implant system details — the brand and model of your implant, if known; many practices provide an implant passport or card at the time of treatment
  • Radiographic records — dental X-rays taken during or after implant placement, particularly any showing bone levels around the implant site
  • Details of your restoration — whether you have a single crown, a bridge supported by implants, or another type of prosthetic
  • Date of implant placement — even an approximate year is helpful
  • Any complications or treatments — details of any issues experienced since the original procedure

Not everyone will have all of this information to hand, and that is entirely understandable. Your previous dental practice may be able to send records on your behalf if you contact them and request a transfer. Under UK data protection guidelines, you are entitled to access your dental records.

If records are not available, a thorough clinical examination and new X-rays at the new practice can still provide a great deal of useful information.

What Does a Dental Implant Review Typically Involve?

Understanding what happens during a dental implant review can help patients feel prepared and informed before they attend. Reviews are generally straightforward appointments, though the level of detail involved may vary depending on how long it has been since the last assessment and whether any concerns are present.

A typical implant review may include:

  • Visual examination of the implant site — the dentist will inspect the gum tissue around the implant crown for any signs of redness, recession, or swelling
  • Probing of the peri-implant tissues — similar to gum health checks on natural teeth, gentle probing around the implant helps assess the health of the surrounding soft tissue
  • Assessment of the restoration — checking that the crown or bridge is secure, undamaged, and functioning correctly with your bite
  • Dental X-rays — periapical X-rays are commonly used to assess bone levels around the implant; these provide a picture of the bone supporting the implant beneath the gum line
  • Occlusal assessment — ensuring the way your teeth meet when you bite or chew is not placing excessive force on the implant

The dentist may also discuss your home care routine, including how you clean around the implant, and whether any adjustments to your technique or tools — such as interdental brushes or implant-specific floss — might be beneficial.

Understanding the Dental Science: Peri-Implant Health

To appreciate why regular reviews matter, it helps to understand a little about the biological environment around a dental implant.

Unlike a natural tooth, which is connected to the jawbone through a network of fibres known as the periodontal ligament, a dental implant is directly integrated into the bone — a process called osseointegration. This is what gives implants their stability and function. However, the tissue immediately surrounding the implant, known as the peri-implant tissue, can be susceptible to inflammation if plaque is allowed to accumulate.

There are two key conditions that clinicians monitor in implant patients:

  • Peri-implant mucositis — inflammation of the soft tissue around the implant, similar in concept to gingivitis around natural teeth. This is generally reversible with improved cleaning and professional treatment.
  • Peri-implantitis — a more advanced inflammatory condition involving bone loss around the implant. If left unaddressed, this can compromise the stability of the implant over time.

Neither of these conditions is inevitable, and the majority of implant patients maintain good peri-implant health with regular professional care and thorough home cleaning. The key point is that early detection through regular reviews offers an early opportunity for any necessary management before changes become more significant.

This is why resuming dental implant aftercare promptly after a move is genuinely worthwhile.

How to Find a New Dental Practice That Provides Implant Care

Not every dental practice offers implant monitoring as part of their routine services, so it is worth confirming this when registering with a new provider. When contacting a practice, you may wish to ask:

  • Whether the practice has experience assessing and maintaining dental implants placed elsewhere
  • Whether they have the equipment for periapical X-rays to assess bone levels around implants
  • Whether any of the dentists hold postgraduate training or qualifications in implant dentistry

When You Should Seek an Earlier Dental Assessment

Most people with dental implants attend routine reviews every six to twelve months, in line with their dentist's recommendation. However, there are certain situations where it would be sensible to arrange a dental assessment sooner rather than waiting for a routine appointment, particularly if you have recently moved and experienced any of the following:

  • Discomfort or sensitivity around an implant — particularly if persistent or worsening
  • Visible changes to the gum tissue — such as swelling, redness, or the gum appearing to pull away from the implant crown
  • A loose or wobbly crown — if the restoration feels less secure than usual, a dentist should assess this promptly
  • Bleeding around the implant site — occasional minor bleeding during cleaning may not be a cause for concern, but persistent or unprovoked bleeding warrants professional attention
  • Any change in your bite — if the way your teeth meet feels different or uncomfortable

None of these signs necessarily indicate a serious problem, but each one is worth having assessed clinically. Dental symptoms and treatment options should always be assessed individually during a clinical examination.

Home Care and Preventative Advice for Dental Implant Patients

Good home care remains one of the most effective ways to protect the long-term health of dental implants. The following practical advice may help you maintain peri-implant health between professional appointments:

Brushing: Use a soft-bristled toothbrush to clean around the implant crown gently but thoroughly, twice daily. Electric toothbrushes can be used safely around implants for most patients.

Interdental cleaning: The spaces between the implant crown and adjacent teeth should be cleaned daily using interdental brushes, implant floss, or a water flosser. Your dentist or hygienist can advise on the most suitable tools for your specific implant configuration.

Avoid habits that place excessive force on the implant: Habits such as grinding teeth, chewing hard objects, or using teeth to open packaging can place unnecessary stress on implant restorations.

Attend a dental hygienist regularly: Professional cleaning around implants, often referred to as peri-implant debridement, helps remove plaque and calculus from areas that are difficult to reach at home.

Maintain overall oral health: The health of your natural teeth and gums also influences the environment around an implant. Treating any gum disease on remaining natural teeth is beneficial for implant longevity.

Key Points to Remember

  • Dental implant aftercare does not end after treatment is complete — ongoing professional reviews are an important part of long-term implant health.
  • Moving to a new area is a good time to re-establish dental care, not a reason to delay it.
  • Gather your implant records before your first appointment with a new practice, including your implant passport if you have one.
  • Inform your new dental practice about your implant history when registering so the appropriate appointment can be arranged.
  • Peri-implant health can be monitored and managed effectively when reviewed regularly by a qualified dental clinician.
  • Seek an earlier assessment if you notice any changes around your implant, rather than waiting for a routine review date.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a dentist review dental implants that were placed at a different practice?

Yes, in most cases a qualified dentist or implant clinician can assess implants that were originally placed elsewhere. They will typically request any available records from your previous practice, and may take new X-rays to establish a current baseline of bone levels around the implant. Whilst it may take a little time to build a complete picture of your implant history, a thorough clinical assessment can provide a great deal of useful information regardless of where treatment was originally carried out.

How soon after moving should I arrange a dental implant review?

It is generally advisable to register with a new dental practice and arrange a review as soon as it is practically possible after moving, particularly if your last assessment was some time ago. If you are overdue a review or have any concerns about your implant, sooner is preferable. Most dental practices aim to see new patients within a few weeks of registration. If you have active symptoms such as discomfort or visible changes around the implant, contact a practice promptly rather than waiting for a routine appointment.

What is an implant passport and do I need one?

An implant passport is a document — often a small card or booklet — provided by the placing clinician at the time of treatment. It records the brand, type, and size of the implant system used. This information can be helpful for future clinicians because different implant systems use different components, and knowing the system used may assist with any future prosthetic work or maintenance. If you do not have an implant passport, your previous dental practice may be able to supply this information from your records.

Is it possible that my implant has been affected during the time I had no dental care?

Many patients experience temporary gaps in dental care due to life circumstances such as moving, and the majority will have no significant complications. Dental implants are generally robust and do not deteriorate quickly. However, without professional monitoring, early changes to the surrounding gum or bone can go undetected for longer than ideal. This is why resuming regular care is important — not because a gap in appointments necessarily means something has gone wrong, but because a thorough assessment provides reassurance and the opportunity to address anything minor before it progresses.

How often should dental implants be professionally reviewed?

The recommended frequency of dental implant reviews varies depending on individual circumstances, including the patient's overall oral health, their home care routine, and any previous history of peri-implant concerns. Many patients are seen every six to twelve months for a combined dental examination and hygienist appointment. Your new dentist will be able to advise on the most appropriate review interval following an initial assessment. Some patients with stable implants and excellent oral health may attend less frequently, whilst others may benefit from more regular appointments.

Will my new dentist be able to repair or adjust my implant crown if needed?

In many cases, a dentist experienced in implant prosthetics can assess and address issues with an existing crown or restoration. The feasibility of this depends on the implant system used, the nature of the issue, and the components available. This is another reason why providing details of your implant system — including the brand, if known — is helpful at your first appointment. If adjustments or replacement of the restoration are needed, your dentist will discuss the options with you based on a clinical assessment.

Conclusion

Moving to a new area presents a natural opportunity to re-establish your dental care and ensure your dental implants continue to receive the professional attention they need. Preparing for a dental implant review after moving does not need to be complicated: gathering your previous records, registering with a practice experienced in implant assessment, and attending for a thorough examination are the key steps.

Ongoing dental implant aftercare — through professional reviews, appropriate X-rays, and consistent home care — is an important part of protecting your long-term oral health and the investment you made in your smile. Whether your implants were placed recently or several years ago, resuming regular monitoring with a qualified clinician is always worthwhile.

If you notice any changes around your implant site in the meantime, arrange an appointment sooner rather than later. Dental symptoms and treatment options should always be assessed individually during a clinical examination.
