Introduction

Many patients find themselves in a situation where they have had a dental implant placed at one clinic — perhaps abroad, through a previous NHS provider, or at a private practice they no longer attend — and are now wondering whether another dentist can review or look after it. This is an entirely understandable concern, and it is one of the most common questions people search for online when it comes to implant aftercare.

Whether you have moved to a new area of London, changed dental practices, or simply feel that your existing implant needs a second opinion, it is reassuring to know that seeking a dental implant check at a different clinic is not only possible — it is often a sensible step. Dental implants require long-term monitoring, and continuity of care is important for your oral health regardless of where the original treatment took place. If you have recently relocated, this guide on preparing for an implant review after moving may also be helpful.

This article explains what a dental implant check involves, why it matters, what signs may suggest your implant needs attention, and how a qualified dental team can support you even if they were not involved in the original placement.

Can You Have a Dental Implant Checked at a Different Clinic?

Yes. A dental implant check can be carried out by a qualified dentist at any reputable clinic, regardless of where the implant was originally placed. The dentist will assess the implant's stability, the surrounding gum tissue, and the bone structure using clinical examination and, where appropriate, dental X-rays. Suitability for ongoing care will depend on individual clinical assessment.

Why Patients Seek Implant Reviews at a Different Clinic

There are many entirely valid reasons why someone might wish to have their dental implant assessed by a different dental practice. These include:

  • Relocation — Moving to a new area of London or to a different part of the UK means your original clinic may no longer be practical to visit.
  • Treatment received abroad — Dental tourism has grown considerably, and many patients return to the UK seeking follow-up care for implants placed overseas.
  • Closure of the original practice — Dental practices do occasionally close, merge, or change ownership, leaving patients without continuity of care.
  • Concern about symptoms — Patients who notice discomfort, swelling, or changes around their implant may seek a second opinion.
  • Lack of ongoing contact — Some patients feel they did not receive adequate aftercare instructions or follow-up appointments after their implant was placed.

All of these are valid and reasonable motivations. No dental professional should discourage you from seeking an assessment elsewhere, and the General Dental Council (GDC) actively supports patient choice in dental care. The most important thing is that your implant receives appropriate monitoring to support long-term oral health.

What Does a Dental Implant Check Involve?

A dental implant check at a new clinic is a thorough clinical review designed to assess the current health and stability of your implant. Although the examining dentist will not have been involved in the original placement, they can still gather detailed information through examination and diagnostic tools.

A typical implant review may include:

  • Visual and clinical examination of the implant crown, abutment, and surrounding soft tissue
  • Probing around the implant site to assess gum pocket depth and check for any signs of inflammation
  • Dental X-rays (periapical or panoramic) to evaluate bone levels and the integrity of the implant fixture beneath the gum
  • Bite assessment to check whether the implant crown is correctly aligned within your occlusion (bite)
  • Review of any symptoms you have been experiencing, including pain, sensitivity, or movement

Where possible, it is helpful to bring any documentation from your original treatment, such as the implant system brand, surgical notes, or X-rays taken at the time of placement. However, a lack of records will not prevent a qualified dentist from conducting a thorough assessment using current clinical findings.

Understanding the Science Behind Dental Implants

To appreciate why regular implant checks matter, it helps to understand a little about how dental implants work from a biological perspective.

A dental implant is a titanium fixture surgically placed into the jawbone to act as a replacement tooth root. Over a period of several months following placement, a process called osseointegration occurs — the surrounding bone tissue grows in close contact with the implant surface, anchoring it securely within the jaw.

Once osseointegration is successful and the implant is stable, an abutment (a small connector piece) is attached, and a custom-made crown is fitted on top to restore the appearance and function of the missing tooth.

However, the long-term success of an implant depends on continued bone support and healthy gum tissue around the implant. The gum and bone surrounding an implant can be affected by peri-implant disease, which refers to inflammatory conditions of the soft and hard tissues around the implant. If left unaddressed, this can lead to bone loss and, in some cases, implant failure over time, often alongside broader periodontal (gum) concerns.

This is precisely why routine monitoring — regardless of which clinic carries it out — is so important for anyone with a dental implant. Early identification of any changes allows for timely intervention and greatly improves the likelihood of a positive long-term outcome.

Signs That Your Dental Implant May Need Attention

Most dental implants, when well maintained and regularly reviewed, function without issue for many years. However, there are certain signs and symptoms that may suggest your implant warrants a clinical assessment sooner rather than later. These are not intended to cause alarm, but rather to help you make an informed decision about when to book an appointment.

Consider seeking a dental implant review if you notice:

  • Discomfort or pain around the implant site, particularly if it persists or has developed some time after the original treatment
  • Swelling or redness in the gum tissue surrounding the implant
  • Bleeding when brushing around the implant area, which may indicate gum inflammation
  • A feeling of movement or looseness in the crown or implant itself
  • Difficulty chewing or a change in how your bite feels
  • A bad taste or persistent bad breath localised around the implant
  • Visible changes to the gum line around the implant, such as the gum appearing to recede

None of these symptoms necessarily indicates a serious problem, and many can be managed effectively when identified early. It is always worth discussing any concerns with a qualified dental professional who can assess the situation based on a full clinical examination.

When Professional Dental Assessment May Be Appropriate

There are specific circumstances in which seeking a professional dental evaluation relatively promptly is advisable. It is important to note that the following guidance is general in nature; individual clinical situations vary, and a thorough examination is always required before any conclusions can be drawn.

You may wish to arrange a dental assessment if:

  • You have not had your implant reviewed in over twelve months
  • You are experiencing any persistent discomfort around the implant site
  • Your gums appear swollen, inflamed, or bleed regularly when you clean around the implant
  • You have noticed a change in how your teeth fit together when you bite
  • You have received an implant abroad and have not yet had a follow-up appointment in the UK
  • Your original dental practice has closed and you have been without ongoing implant monitoring

A calm and measured approach is usually most appropriate. Many of the above scenarios are straightforward to manage when assessed early. Delaying review may, in some cases, limit the treatment options available, so it is generally sensible to seek guidance sooner rather than later if you have concerns.

What to Bring to Your Implant Review Appointment

Whilst a qualified dentist can assess your implant without prior records, providing as much information as possible about your original treatment helps the reviewing clinician build a more complete picture of your case.

Helpful information to bring if available:

  • The name of the implant system used (e.g. Nobel Biocare, Straumann, Osstem) — this may be on your treatment paperwork or discharge summary
  • X-rays taken before, during, or after implant placement
  • Treatment notes or a discharge summary from the placing clinic
  • Information about when the implant was placed and whether there were any complications noted at the time
  • A list of any medications you are currently taking, as these can sometimes affect bone health and gum tissue

If you do not have access to any of this information, do not be deterred from attending. A new clinic can take updated X-rays and conduct a comprehensive examination based on current clinical findings.

Ongoing Care and Maintenance for Dental Implants

One of the most effective ways to protect the long-term health of a dental implant is through consistent daily oral hygiene and regular professional maintenance. Implants themselves cannot decay, but the gum tissue and bone supporting them require the same level of care as natural teeth — arguably more so in some respects, given that implants do not have the same biological warning signals as natural teeth when problems begin to develop.

Daily oral hygiene recommendations for implant patients:

  • Brush twice daily using a soft-bristled toothbrush and fluoride toothpaste, paying careful attention to the gum margins around the implant
  • Use interdental brushes or floss specifically designed for use around implants to clean beneath the crown and around the abutment
  • Consider an oral irrigator (water flosser) to help flush debris from hard-to-reach areas around the implant
  • Avoid smoking, which significantly increases the risk of peri-implant disease and implant failure
  • Attend regular professional hygiene appointments — most implant patients benefit from hygiene visits at least twice per year, though your dentist may recommend a different frequency based on your individual circumstances

Implants Placed Abroad: What UK Patients Should Know

Dental tourism — travelling overseas to receive treatment at a lower cost — has become increasingly common among UK patients. Countries such as Hungary, Turkey, and Poland are popular destinations for dental implant treatment, and many patients return with successful results. However, a significant number also return without adequate follow-up arrangements in place.

If you have had an implant placed abroad, it is strongly advisable to register with a UK dental practice and arrange a review appointment. This allows a qualified UK dentist to:

  • Establish a baseline assessment of your implant's current status
  • Take updated X-rays for comparison in future reviews
  • Advise on appropriate hygiene and maintenance
  • Identify and manage any early signs of concern before they develop further

Documentation from overseas clinics can sometimes be incomplete or in a different language, but a thorough clinical examination at a UK clinic can provide a useful starting point for your ongoing care. You should always inform your reviewing dentist of any known details about the implant system used.

It is also worth noting that UK dental professionals operate under GDC regulatory standards, which set clear expectations for clinical care, patient communication, and professional accountability.

Key Points to Remember

  • You can have a dental implant reviewed at any reputable dental clinic, regardless of where it was originally placed.
  • A thorough dental implant check includes clinical examination, gum probing, X-rays, and bite assessment.
  • Regular implant monitoring — ideally annually or as advised by your dentist — supports long-term implant health.
  • Signs such as persistent discomfort, gum swelling, or a loose-feeling crown are worth discussing with a dental professional promptly.
  • Patients who received implants abroad should arrange a follow-up review with a UK dentist as soon as possible.
  • Good daily oral hygiene and professional hygiene appointments are essential for maintaining the health of the tissue surrounding your implant.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will a dentist be able to tell which implant system was used?

In some cases, a dentist may be able to identify the implant brand from X-rays, particularly if the design characteristics are recognisable. However, this is not always possible without manufacturer records. If the exact system cannot be confirmed, this will primarily be relevant if prosthetic components (such as the abutment or crown) need replacing in the future. For routine monitoring and hygiene maintenance, the exact brand is not always essential. It is still worth sharing any documentation you have from your original clinic to help the reviewing dentist build as complete a picture as possible.

Can another dentist treat a problem with my implant if they didn't place it?

Yes, in many cases a qualified dentist or implant specialist can manage problems such as peri-implant disease, a loose crown, or soft tissue concerns, even if they were not involved in the original placement. The ability to intervene will depend on the nature of the problem and the individual clinical situation. Some complex cases — particularly those involving the implant fixture itself — may require input from a specialist. Your reviewing dentist will advise you on the most appropriate course of action following examination.

How often should a dental implant be checked?

Most dental professionals recommend that implants are reviewed at least once per year as part of a routine dental examination. However, the frequency of checks may be higher for patients who have had previous complications, those with a history of gum disease, or those who smoke. Your dentist will recommend a monitoring schedule based on your individual needs and clinical findings. In between formal reviews, professional hygiene appointments are also recommended to maintain the health of the gum tissue around the implant.

Is it normal to feel discomfort around a dental implant years after placement?

Some mild sensitivity during healing is expected in the weeks immediately following implant placement. However, discomfort that develops or persists long after the implant has fully integrated is not considered normal and should be assessed by a dental professional. In many cases, late-onset discomfort around an implant may be related to peri-implant inflammation or an issue with the crown or bite alignment, both of which can often be managed when identified early. It is always advisable to seek a clinical assessment rather than waiting to see whether symptoms resolve on their own.

What is peri-implantitis, and should I be concerned about it?

Peri-implantitis is an inflammatory condition that affects the gum tissue and bone surrounding a dental implant. It is similar in some respects to periodontitis (gum disease) in natural teeth and can, if left unmanaged, lead to bone loss around the implant. Risk factors include poor oral hygiene, smoking, a history of gum disease, and infrequent professional monitoring. It is not a reason for alarm, particularly when identified at an early stage — but it does highlight the importance of regular implant reviews and professional hygiene support. A dentist can assess whether any signs of peri-implant disease are present and advise accordingly.

Can I register with a new dental practice specifically for my implant care?

Yes. You are entirely within your rights as a patient to register with a new dental practice for your ongoing care, including implant monitoring. Many patients in London and across the UK do this when they relocate, when their existing clinic closes, or simply when they feel another practice may better suit their needs. When registering, let the practice know that you have a dental implant and request that an implant review is included as part of your initial new-patient examination. You can also request that your records be transferred from your previous clinic where this is possible. If you are ready to arrange this, you can book an appointment online with our London clinic.

Conclusion

Having a dental implant checked at a different clinic is not only possible — it is a perfectly reasonable and often very sensible decision. Whether you have moved, received treatment abroad, lost contact with your original clinic, or simply want reassurance about the state of your implant, a qualified dental team can conduct a thorough clinical assessment and advise you on the most appropriate next steps.

Dental implants are a long-term investment in your oral health, and like all dental restorations, they benefit from regular professional monitoring and attentive daily care. Keeping up with routine reviews, maintaining excellent oral hygiene, and seeking timely advice when concerns arise all contribute to the long-term success of an implant.

If you have any concerns about your dental implant or would like to arrange a review, we encourage you to speak with a qualified dental professional at your earliest convenience.

Dental symptoms and treatment options should always be assessed individually during a clinical examination.
